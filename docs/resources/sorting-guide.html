Three bins, one habit

Compost Green bin

  • Food scraps and peels
  • Coffee grounds and filters
  • Napkins and soiled paper
  • Approved compostable serviceware
  • Tea bags and plant trimmings

Recycle Blue bin

  • Bottles and aluminum cans
  • Clean paper and cardboard
  • Rigid plastics #1 and #2
  • Glass containers

Empty and rinse, please.

Landfill Black bin

  • Foil-lined wrappers
  • Soft or mixed plastics
  • Coated paper cups

Last resort only. When in doubt, use landfill, then ask.

Know your plastics

#1 #2Recycle
#3 to #7Landfill

Check the number inside the recycling symbol on the bottom of plastic packaging.

Looks compostable. Goes to landfill.

"Compostable" PLA cups (#7) and "plant-based" CPLA cutlery go in the landfill bin at Gateway. Many departments buy these with good intentions.

If single-use is truly necessary, buy the paper- and fiber-based compostable items listed in the Approved Products Catalog. Reusables still come first.

The Science

PLA is a plastic made from plants. It only breaks down in high-heat industrial composting, which runs at around 58°C (136°F). Gateway's compost doesn't accept it, and because it looks like regular plastic, it contaminates the bins.

Source: Oh et al., "Structural evaluation of poly(lactic acid) degradation at standardized composting temperature of 58 degrees," Chemosphere, 2024

Why no desk-side bins?

Gateway uses centralized waste stations instead. They improve sorting, reduce contamination, and cut down on plastic bin liners, in line with the campus-wide move away from desk-side collection.

Bring your waste to the stations. Batteries and e-waste have their own pathways on 04 Recovery Systems.
