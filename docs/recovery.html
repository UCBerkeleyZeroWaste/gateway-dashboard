Recovery Systems

Some materials can't be reused. We built pathways so they still stay out of landfill.

Reuse and refill handle most of what moves through Gateway. Recovery is the last stop for what's left: food scraps, electronics, and batteries.

Recovery pathways

Three streams, each with its own way out of the building.

Compost

Compost bins at the waste stations, plus the Mill in the 1st floor social kitchen.

What goes in

Takes: food scraps, meat and bones, dairy, coffee grounds and filters, tea bags, napkins, and approved compostable serviceware.

Keeps food out of landfill and cuts methane.

E-waste

Collect old electronics in your department. When the collection is full, email Sarah Pecora to arrange a pickup.

What goes in

Takes: cables, chargers, electronics, and old devices.

A certified recycler keeps metals and hazardous materials out of landfill.

Email Sarah for a pickup

Batteries

Bottom shelf of the ReUse Station, in the 2nd floor southwest social kitchen.

What goes in

Takes: AA, AAA, 9V, button cell, and rechargeable batteries.

A certified recycler recovers the metals and keeps them out of groundwater.

The Science

Food buried in a landfill breaks down without oxygen and releases methane. Over 100 years, methane traps roughly 28 times as much heat as the same amount of CO2. Composting keeps that from happening.

Source: U.S. EPA, Understanding Global Warming Potentials (methane: 27 to 30 over 100 years)

Meet the Mill

Most pathways are a labeled bin. The Mill is different: it's a food recycler in the 1st floor social kitchen that dries and grinds scraps overnight into nutrient-rich grounds.
